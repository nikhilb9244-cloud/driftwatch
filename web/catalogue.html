How orbit groups are defined

Low Earth: apogee below 2,000 km. Geostationary band: within 200 km of 35,786 km. Highly elliptical: eccentricity above 0.25. Medium Earth lies between the low and geostationary bands; other covers remaining transfer, graveyard and distant orbits. These are catalogue classifications, not live altitude filters.

A view of what public orbital data predicts — and where that prediction needs care.

01

Positions are calculated

SGP4 is an orbit model. It carries published orbital elements forward or back to the time the selection specifies. The moving points are predictions, not live measurements.

02

More detail is not more certainty

Error varies by object, age of the elements, atmospheric conditions and manoeuvres. A crisp dot does not establish a precise position. Dots are enlarged for visibility.

03

Consistency is not accuracy

The risk model estimates uncertainty from differences between successive element sets. Those sets can share errors. Their agreement does not establish the true orbit.

Read a collision probability

A probability combines the predicted separation, assumed object sizes and position uncertainty. Each can change the answer. These public-data estimates are indicative.

In the dilution region, reducing uncertainty while holding the predicted separation fixed can raise the probability. A small number there does not establish safety. Better tracking would change both the uncertainty and the predicted separation.

The robust region describes the model's response to covariance scaling. It does not certify a reliable orbit or an operational probability.

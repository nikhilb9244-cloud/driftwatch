## Abstract

Public SGP4 element sets are compared with reconstructed orbits for fifteen spacecraft at 460 to 1,338 km over a quiet window and three storm windows, with 1,201 usable sets and laser ranging as a further reference. At 460 to 507 km, the horizon for keeping the 95th-percentile in-track residual inside 25 km is five days in quiet conditions, two in May, one in October and two in August; higher bands generally retain longer horizons. Covariance fitted from successive-set consistency under-covers storm errors and also fails in quiet conditions at 1,338 km. A storm term driven by observed ap has skill that depends on lead and coefficient source. The published ML-dSGP4 recipe lowers its training loss but improves no held-out lead at this data volume. Post-burn errors reveal fits whose epochs have advanced beyond burns they do not contain. Independent checks expose convention and provenance faults that internal consistency misses. For the lowest measured band at L band, whether a satellite crossed a telescope beam is known for seven days, while its position at an instant is known for hours. These are measurements on the stated population, not operational guarantees.

## Related work

Related work connects the covariance, storm, learned-propagator and radio questions. [Flohrer, Krag and Klinkrad (2008)](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2008/Orbital_Debris/Flohrer.pdf) assess TLE orbit uncertainty through catalogue consistency, which cannot reveal errors shared by the fits. [Parker and Linares (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08617) infer the Gannon storm's density enhancement from catalogue decay and assess ap forecast performance. [Acciarini, Baydin and Izzo (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2024.10.063) introduce differentiable SGP4 and learn corrections against operator ephemerides. [Cerardi, Tolley and di Vruno (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22694) forecast satellite occupancy of SKA observing beams. The measurements here test consistency-derived covariance and propagation against reconstructed orbits, separate observed-ap correction skill from forecasting, evaluate the published hybrid recipe at the available public-data volume, and distinguish knowing that a satellite crossed a beam from knowing its position at an instant.

## 1. What was measured, and on what population

Every public element set issued inside a one-week window is one trial: it is propagated with SGP4 to leads from six hours to seven days and compared with the spacecraft's reconstructed orbit in the spacecraft's radial, in-track, cross-track frame, one residual per lead. The covariance beside each set and the ballistic coefficient the storm term uses are fitted from the 45 and 36 days of history before the window and from nothing inside it. Four windows: a control from 20 to 27 April 2024 with Kp at or under 4; the May 2024 storm, sets issued 6 to 13 May; the 10 to 11 October 2024 storm (Kp 9-), held out from every tuning; the 12 August 2024 storm (Kp 8-), held out in the same way ([Swarm benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/calibration-benchmark.md) and [reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)).

The population is fifteen spacecraft whose reconstructed orbit a server hands out without an account, Swarm A, B and C, GRACE-FO 1 and 2, Sentinel-1A and the DORIS satellites CryoSat-2, SARAL, Sentinel-3A and 3B, SWOT, HY-2C and 2D, Jason-3 and Sentinel-6A, at 460 to 1,338 km, near-circular, free-flying between manoeuvres. Manoeuvre arcs are excluded from a published thruster record where one exists (Swarm, GRACE-FO) and from a step detector on the reconstructed orbit otherwise, with the 24 hours before each set's epoch counted on both paths. 1,201 element sets are scored. Laser ranging is a second reference for every mission with a retroreflector; the two references agree at the metre level, with median range residuals of −0.1 to −0.6 m for every mission but SWOT, whose residual is a constant −2.0 m from the uncorrected retroreflector offset from the centre of mass ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)). Nothing is measured for debris, eccentric orbits, station-kept constellations, objects the network tracks less often, or above 1,340 km.

The horizon is stated for one task, keeping the spacecraft inside the 25 km in-track half-width of the screening box at the 95th percentile of trials ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)):

| Band (mean altitude of the sets) | Spacecraft | quiet | May 2024 | October 2024 | August 2024 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 460 to 507 km | Swarm A, B, C; GRACE-FO 1, 2 | 5 d (95) | 2 d (91) | 24 h (100) | 2 d (93) |
| 696 to 719 km | Sentinel-1A, CryoSat-2 | 5 d (45) | 5 d (44) | 7 d (41) | 5 d (39) |
| 783 to 803 km | SARAL, Sentinel-3A, 3B | 7 d (57) | 7 d (68) | 5 d (51) | 7 d (60) |
| 893 to 952 km | SWOT, HY-2C, 2D | 7 d (78) | 7 d (77) | 7 d (60) | 7 d (65) |
| 1,338 km | Jason-3, Sentinel-6A | 7 d (37) | 7 d (33) | 7 d (32) | 7 d (35) |

Parentheses give the usable sets per band and window at six hours; counts can fall at longer leads as propagation arcs encounter burns or reference coverage ends, and the [per-lead counts](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md#the-in-track-residual-by-band-window-and-lead) accompany the residuals. Successive sets share tracking information and overlapping propagation arcs, so the trials are correlated; on seven to ten usable sets a mission's 95th percentile is a rank statistic near the maximum, controlled by the largest residuals rather than a well-resolved tail probability.

On Swarm alone ([Swarm benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/calibration-benchmark.md); 57, 54 and 61 sets in three windows) the median in-track residual at seven days is 24, 75 and 49 km in the quiet, May and October windows and the 95th percentile 62, 197 and 652 km. GRACE-FO, at 490 km, agrees with Swarm to within one lead bin in every window, at 6 days, 2 to 3 days, 24 to 36 hours and 2 days: the Swarm result reproduced on a second spacecraft with another producer's orbit and another thruster record ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)). Both the Swarm and GRACE-FO references are GNSS-derived reconstructed orbits: the independence is in producer, mission and thruster record, and laser ranging is the check independent of measurement type. At 700 km a storm no longer shortens the horizon below the quiet week's five days; at 800 km and above the horizon is seven days in every window but October at 800 km, 5 days, carried by SARAL at 4 and Sentinel-3A at 5.

One correction to the table is dated 2026-09-07. The record path had from the first run dropped every set with a burn in the 24 hours before its epoch; the detection path had dropped only pairs whose propagation arc crossed a burn. The rule was extended to the detection path without change, after the held-out results had been seen. It moved the 750 to 850 km October horizon from 3 days to 5 and the 600 to 750 km August horizon from 6 days to 5; the 460 to 507 km row did not move; the usable sets went from 1,228 to 1,201. The held-out figures in the two bands carry that qualification, and the tables as they stood before the correction are kept on the pages ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)).

SpaceX's published Starlink ephemerides are a separate source: 72-hour operator predictions carrying planned burns and the operator's drag model, to which CelesTrak fits supplemental element sets ([SpaceX comparison](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/spacex-ephemerides.md)). The published 0.20 km residual is measured over the fitting arc, not over the full 72-hour file. The files have a seam at exactly 48 hours: the position steps by a few hundred metres and the published velocity disagrees with the central difference of the positions by 16 m/s, at the same lead in ten of ten files, then nineteen of nineteen ([SpaceX seam](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/spacex-ephemerides.md); measured 2026-09-03). The header labels the product `blend`; a join between arcs is the likely explanation, and an interpolant must not span it.

On nineteen matched files measured 2026-09-03, the propagated supplemental set differs from the published states by the following median distances, almost all along track ([SpaceX comparison](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/spacex-ephemerides.md)):

| Lead from the file's start | under 12 h | 12 to 24 h | 24 to 36 h | 36 to 48 h | 48 to 60 h | 60 to 72 h |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Median distance | 0.30 km | 2.8 km | 11.5 km | 28.3 km | 51.8 km | 82.9 km |

This is one date and nineteen satellites, and the far-end figure is one bin of the table, against an operator's published prediction rather than a realised orbit. The same-file lineage check reproduces the drift, so revisions of the plan between files do not explain it; planned manoeuvres within a file and fit noise in mean motion remain possible causes. The comparison cannot tell whether the fit or the operator's prediction was nearer the realised trajectory ([lineage and limits](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/spacex-ephemerides.md#lineage-qualification)).

## 2. Calibration: the storm under-coverage, and the failure at 1,338 km

The covariance is fitted from the consistency of an object's successive element sets and claims 95 per cent of residuals inside two sigma. On Swarm ([Swarm benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/calibration-benchmark.md)) it over-covers in the quiet week from one to five days and under-covers inside twelve hours; in both storms it under-covers at every lead, two sigma containing 65 to 80 per cent of the May residuals and 62 to 75 per cent of the October ones. On the fifteen spacecraft ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)) the 460 to 507 km band repeats this, at 98 to 100 per cent inside two sigma in the quiet week, 65 to 84 per cent in the storms and 33 per cent at seven days in August; above 600 km the storms under-cover at a day, at 67 to 85 per cent; the six-hour under-coverage is general, at 17 to 57 per cent in every band.

The explanation recorded on 2026-09-05 ([Swarm benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/calibration-benchmark.md)) has two parts. A covariance fitted from the consistency of quiet fits cannot describe storm-time error, because nothing in how quiet fits disagree with one another says how far a storm will move the object. A second mechanism has a measured size: the fit excludes intervals that span a detected burn, the detector reads an unexplained change in semi-major axis as a burn, and storm drag is such a change; among 17 free-flying objects whose stored history spans the 10 to 13 May 2024 storm, 2, both debris, had the storm interval excluded as a burn. Nothing in the detector was changed.

At 1,338 km the covariance fails in the quiet week as well ([high-altitude calibration](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/findings.md#9-at-1338-km-the-consistency-derived-covariance-under-covers-in-quiet-conditions-because-the-sets-agree-with-one-another-better-than-with-the-truth)), on Jason-3 and Sentinel-6A, in-track, inside two sigma:

| Window | Median in-track residual at a day | Fitted one-day sigma | Inside two sigma at a day | At six hours |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| quiet | 0.27 km | 0.26 km | 51 % | 32 % |
| May 2024 | 0.45 km | 0.11 km | 6 % | 15 % |
| October 2024 | 0.32 km | 0.43 km | 81 % | 53 % |
| August 2024 | 0.27 km | 0.04 km | 9 % | 0 % |
| 400 to 600 km, quiet, for comparison | 0.37 km | 0.98 km | 98 % | |

The sets agree with one another to less than their error. Drag is negligible at that altitude, so successive fits by the same network to the same tracking repeat one another, and the error they share is invisible to any measure of their consistency: the fitted sigma states the network's repeatability, which coincides with its accuracy only where a changing atmosphere makes successive fits disagree by about as much as they are wrong, at 500 km in a quiet week and nowhere in a storm. The fix recorded, a covariance scale per altitude band validated on the held-out windows, is applied on no page. Two decisions were recorded on 2026-09-05 and not made: a quiet-condition baseline for the storm term, which wants a diagnosis before anything is zeroed, and a storm-conditional covariance scale, to be fitted on May 2024 and validated on October 2024 or it is a tuning on the number it is meant to predict ([Swarm benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/calibration-benchmark.md)).

## 3. The storm term: skill by lead and by coefficient source, and the withdrawn headlines

The storm term is the in-track shift from the density excess over what a set's own drag term implies, driven by the observed ap and a ballistic coefficient. Its storm-week test ([storm-week test](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/storm-validation.md)) propagates each object's last pre-storm set through the May 2024 storm and compares it with the sets issued during the storm, on the live catalogue; a comparison is validated only where both coefficients were measured from the objects' own decay, 101 objects and 498 comparisons on the redrawn sample. The term has skill only for that population and none where the coefficient is a B* inversion or a population stand-in. The skill sits at three to four days of lead and is near zero inside two (recomputed 2026-09-05, reproduced on a redrawn sample the same day): the observed sign agrees with the predicted one on 39 and 41 per cent of comparisons at one and two days, chance being 50, and on 91 and 96 per cent at three and four days. NRLMSIS 2.1 over-predicts the storm's three-day density enhancement by 22 to 23 per cent over 450 to 2,000 km; recorded, not applied. This figure compares a three-day integral inferred from orbital decay with the model at fixed altitude, not an accelerometer's instantaneous peak, and does not contradict published under-prediction of that peak ([comparison of the quantities](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/storm-validation.md#the-two-sides-are-not-measuring-the-same-quantity-and-here-is-each-one-written-out)).

Against the reconstructed orbits ([Swarm benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/calibration-benchmark.md), Swarm) the term with the observed ap reduces the May residual only from four days of lead, by 20 to 48 per cent on the median, and increases it from twelve hours to three days; its magnitude exceeds the drift, a median predicted shift of 100 km at seven days against an actual 64. In the held-out October storm it helps from six hours to five days, by 5 to 43 per cent, and hurts at six and seven days. In the quiet control it makes the residual worse from one to six days, by 10 to 96 per cent, because the excess it integrates is not zero without a storm. On the fifteen spacecraft ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)) the same holds at 460 to 507 km; above 700 km its effect is within about 30 per cent either way with no consistent sign, and at 1,338 km it is nil, because the coefficient it needs is fitted from decay that is not measurable there.

Corrected 2026-09-05 ([storm-week test](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/storm-validation.md)): the term must not be applied to operator-controlled objects, whose trajectory already carries the operator's drag model and planned burns. Before the correction every object with a coefficient was displaced, which put shifts of up to 31,000 km on Starlinks whose supplemental B* described a thrusting plan, 42 objects on the 1 September run and 36 on the 3 September one, explained at the time as a physical population in the densest shell. That explanation was wrong.

Three headlines were withdrawn ([withdrawn headlines](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/findings.md#5-three-withdrawn-headlines)). "A storm lowers the probability on most events, because the two objects are displaced alike": on 2026-09-03 the relative displacement that reaches the miss was found to be a median 1.91 times the mean of the two objects' own displacements, out of a possible 2; on 2026-09-05 the lowering itself was found to live in events with an operator-controlled side, and on the 981 free-flying events of the 3 September run the probability is lowered on 55 and raised on 43, at a median ratio of 0.98. "Screening on the operator's own published states gives the demo fleet its one red flag", corrected 2026-09-05: the flag, EOS SAT-1 against Starlink 61705 at 2.780 km and a fifteen-hour lead, is in the dilution region at low confidence, its maximum probability over covariance scale factors sitting at 0.85 times the covariance in hand. "The storm term is predictive at r = 0.88 where the coefficient is measured", withdrawn 2026-09-05: a redrawn sample gave 0.64, the correlation being carried by the two per cent of predictions with the most weight. No correlation is quoted.

## 4. The dSGP4 held-out result

ESA's dsgp4 (Acciarini, Baydin and Izzo 2025; version 1.3.0) was run on the 1,201 usable trial sets ([dSGP4 evaluation](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/dsgp4-evaluation.md)). With WGS72 constants it reproduces the sgp4 library's in-track residual to the metre at every lead in every window. Its ML-dSGP4 hybrid wraps the propagator in two networks that perturb the mean elements before the propagation and the state after it, trained by the mean squared error of the normalised state; the published training used an operator's predictions in a quiet week. Two hybrids were trained on the quiet and May windows only, with the corrections starting at zero so that the untrained model is SGP4 and the epoch of lowest training loss kept: one on Swarm (111 sets, 18,648 hourly reference states) and one on every mission with a reconstructed orbit (625 sets, 105,000 hourly reference states). The published dataset contained about 6.5 million state rows from 1,519 Starlinks, with 69 per cent used for training ([published experiment](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04830v5#S4.SS2)). This result tests the recipe at a public-data user's data volume. The training lowers the published objective and raises the residual: on the training windows the Swarm hybrid's median in-track residual is 7 to 46 km against SGP4's 0.4 to 7 km, and on the held-out storms neither hybrid improves one of the ten leads, at a day in October 15 and 5 km against 0.64 and in August 14 and 7 km against 0.45. The rule fixed before the numbers were seen, adopt only if the held-out storms improve, says do not adopt. The objective, a mean of squared errors over every hour to seven days, is dominated by the seven-day tail, so its optimum trades the short leads for a change at the tail: a loss that fell by two per cent moved the day-one residual by an order of magnitude. This does not say the hybrid cannot be made to help; it says the published recipe, on public element sets against reconstructed orbits, did not.

## 5. The post-burn mechanism

In the first run of the expansion Sentinel-3B's October horizon was 3 days against Sentinel-3A's 7 in the same orbit. The cause was the exclusion rule of section 1, corrected 2026-09-07: every short 3B window was carried by the one set issued a few hours after that window's burn, 33 km wrong at four days in October, and with 7 to 10 usable sets per window one such set is the 95th percentile. Why 3B's first post-burn sets were wrong by tens of kilometres and 3A's were not was recorded as open ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)).

The property the two differ in, the cadence of their sets (one every 6.7 hours for 3A, one every 10 to 13 hours for 3B), was tested on 2026-09-08 on every mission with a burn inside a window's set span and a set issued after it: twelve burns on six spacecraft at 700 to 950 km, Sentinel-1A, CryoSat-2, Sentinel-3A, Sentinel-3B, SWOT and HY-2D, in all four windows. The rank correlation of the first post-burn set's in-track residual at four days with cadence is +0.78 per burn (n = 12) and is Sentinel-3B alone: without its three burns it is +0.48 on nine (p = 0.19), and with the spacecraft as the unit +0.66 on six (p = 0.16). Error does not scale with cadence on this population. The seven first sets issued within ten hours of the burn are wrong at four days by 2.3 to 33 km and the five issued twelve hours or later by 0.1 to 2.3 km, on twelve burns of six spacecraft in four windows, a split re-measured whenever a window is added; burn size, 9 to 95 m of semi-major axis, orders nothing.

The mechanism was found the same day by asking what fit each set carries ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)). Each set's semi-major axis was put in the reconstructed orbit's convention, SGP4 over one revolution centred on its epoch with the osculating value averaged as the detector averages the orbit's; the constant between the two conventions, 70 to 74 m for every mission and most of it the ten-second finite-difference velocity the orbit reader carries, was calibrated on each window's sets with no burn in their arc, whose scatter is 2 to 6 m; and the set's error was measured against the orbit at its own epoch, because CryoSat-2 at 717 km decays about 10 m a day and had moved 25 m below the post-burn plateau by the time its sets were issued. The fraction of the burn a set contains is one plus its error over the burn. The thresholds, a quarter and three quarters, and the rule that a burn under three scatters is unresolved, were fixed before any set was classified.

| Spacecraft | Burns | Fraction of the burn in the first set after it | Class |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Sentinel-3B | 3 | 0.06, 0.04, 0.12 | pre-burn fit, epoch advanced past the burn |
| SWOT | 1 | −0.07 | pre-burn fit, epoch advanced past the burn |
| CryoSat-2 | 3 | 0.92, 1.06, 0.85 | contains the burn |
| Sentinel-3A | 2 | 1.02, 0.98 | contains the burn |
| HY-2D | 2 | 0.86, 1.00 | contains the burn |
| Sentinel-1A | 1 | −0.22 | unresolved: a 9 m burn against a 15 m threshold |

No set lies between the two classes. For the four re-epoched sets the drift their error predicts, three halves of the mean motion times the error times the lead, reproduces the observed residual at four days to within 20 per cent (−21, −34, −25 and −17 km predicted against −25, −33, −30 and −19 observed). So, on twelve burns of six spacecraft in four windows: the first set after a burn is wrong by the part of the burn it does not contain, and whether it contains the burn or is a pre-burn fit re-epoched past it differs by object, not by delay. CryoSat-2's set issued 5.4 hours after its burn contained it; Sentinel-3B's issued 3 to 8 hours after did not. The ten-hour split describes this sample, in which the re-epoched sets were the ones issued within ten hours. What stays open is why the network re-epochs a pre-burn fit for some objects and refits after the burn for others; the sets do not say. Two candidate explanations are that usable post-burn tracking had not yet arrived, or that the catalogue's fit and update policy had not yet incorporated tracking already available; the published sets cannot distinguish them. Nothing in a re-epoched set reveals what it is; the jump appears only when a later set contains the burn, so a per-object mark of the condition is retrospective (`docs/radio-lane.md`).

## 6. The radio horizons: crossing and position

The beam assumptions use the 13.5 m dish and a half-power full width of 1.13 lambda/D, converted to degrees and scaled inversely with frequency: 1.76 degrees at the UHF centre, 816 MHz; 1.12 degrees at the L-band centre, 1284 MHz; 0.66 degrees at the S0 centre, 2187.5 MHz; and 0.47 degrees at the S4 centre, 3062.5 MHz. The tolerance is a third of that half-power width. Zenith range, equal to altitude, gives the largest angular error for a given spatial residual and is the worst-case geometry used for the horizons; lower elevations reduce the angle. The S-band statement below is at 3.5 GHz, where the width is 0.41 degrees; at 1.75 GHz the same scaling gives twice that width ([beam model and receiver widths](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/radio-horizon.md#the-beam)).

The radio lane ([radio horizons](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/radio-horizon.md)) converts the benchmark's residuals into angles at the MeerKAT array centre and tests them against a third of the primary beam's half-power width, per altitude band of section 1, each object scored against its own band's trials. The crossing horizon, governed by the cross-track error, answers whether an object crossed the beam during an observation: the cross-track residual stays under 3.4 arcmin at the 95th percentile at every lead in every window in every band, so the crossing horizon is seven days in every band and window at every receiver, with one exception, 5 days at 600 to 750 km in August 2024; the crossing's time is known to the along-track shift, 0.2 s at six hours at 400 to 600 km and 8 to 72 s at seven days. The position horizon, governed by the along-track error, answers where an object is at an instant to within a third of the beam, and it is the one a storm moves and altitude lengthens. At the L-band centre:

| Band | quiet | May 2024 | October 2024 | August 2024 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 400 to 600 km | 24 h | 12 h | 6 h | 12 h |
| 600 to 750 km | 24 h | 24 h | 12 h | 36 h |
| 750 to 850 km | 6 d | 2 d | 6 h | 3 d |
| 850 to 1,000 km | 7 d | 7 d | 3 d | 7 d |
| 1,338 km | 7 d | 3 d | 7 d | 7 d |

At 3.5 GHz, S-band position prediction from public element sets is not possible at any measured element-set age at 400 to 600 km: at the top of the band the position horizon is under six hours, the benchmark's shortest lead, in every window. Higher it is possible: at the top of S band, 12 hours in the quiet and May windows at 600 to 750 km, 3 days in the quiet week at 750 to 850 km, seven days in the quiet week at 850 to 1,000 km with 12 hours to 4 days in the storms, and seven days in every window but May (36 hours) at 1,338 km. Both horizons rest on the population of section 1; every object outside it carries no measured horizon for either.

## 7. The format and provenance faults, as a class

Six faults were internally consistent and invisible to every pipeline check then in place. Each was exposed by a reference independent of the calculation or claim being checked. The spatial errors are ordered by magnitude, followed by the test and input coverage errors, ordered by the fraction of the claimed total affected.

| Fault | Magnitude | How it was exposed | Dated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ESA's Swarm orbits are in GPS time, which the SP3 header declares and the first reader did not honour ([provenance corrections](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/findings.md#2-the-published-files-are-in-a-different-frame-from-the-one-the-catalogue-uses-and-only-the-filename-says-so)) | a constant 137 km in-track offset at every lead, 18 s at 7.6 km/s | a residual against the reconstructed orbit that refused to grow with lead, while the element sets agreed with each other as before and every test passed | corrected 2026-09-05 |
| SpaceX's states are in MEME (J2000); the header names the covariance frame and never the states' ([provenance corrections](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/findings.md#2-the-published-files-are-in-a-different-frame-from-the-one-the-catalogue-uses-and-only-the-filename-says-so)) | 0.36 degrees from TEME by 2026, about 44 km at low Earth orbit radius; read as TEME the states sit a median 36.2 km from CelesTrak's fit to the same file, rotated into TEME 0.356 km | comparing the states with the fit to the same file, on six satellites' files; every fetch re-runs the comparison | 2026-09-03 |
| The orbit reader's ten-second finite-difference velocity is a chord velocity, lower than the instantaneous velocity, so the vis-viva semi-major axis is biased low ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md); `src/driftwatch/storm/precise.py`) | most of the constant 70 to 74 m difference between the element sets' and reconstructed orbits' mean semi-major axes, against 2 to 6 m scatter on clean sets | comparing the two orbit representations on clean arcs, then reproducing the offset on a designed orbit with known velocity; the post-burn comparison calibrates it away (`tests/test_precise.py`) | calibrated 2026-09-08 |
| SLRF2020 locates a laser station's ground marker; the telescope's reference point is offset from it, and the first reader omitted the separately published ILRS site eccentricity ([provenance corrections](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/findings.md#2-the-published-files-are-in-a-different-frame-from-the-one-the-catalogue-uses-and-only-the-filename-says-so)) | −1 to −3.5 m at four NASA MOBLAS stations, growing with elevation, on every satellite in every window; 3.18 m upward at Yarragadee | comparing the two references per station rather than per mission; the per-mission medians are now −0.1 to −0.6 m | corrected 2026-09-07 |
| The Fortran model behind pymsis truncates its parameter-file path at 128 characters; when the file cannot be found, a plain `STOP` terminates the interpreter with status 0 before pytest can report (`scripts/check_test_count.py`) | about 66 of 594 tests ran, leaving about 89 per cent unrun while the shell reported success | counting completed tests against the collected suite independently of the exit status; CI now requires a final JUnit report whose count matches a fresh collection and a minimum floor | guarded 2026-09-07 |
| A live run's covariance fit read every stored element set for its objects and labelled the result with the 45-day window ([provenance corrections](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/findings.md#2-the-published-files-are-in-a-different-frame-from-the-one-the-catalogue-uses-and-only-the-filename-says-so)) | 615,648 of 2,714,544 sets lay before the window; refitted with the bound, the one-day in-track sigma changed on 21,644 of 22,039 objects (median −5 per cent), 21 flagged events became 12, and the median ratio of probabilities after to before is 0.98 | the fit's inputs counted against its label, which had been written from the configuration rather than from the data | corrected 2026-09-05 |

The documentation bulk-substitution was the same class in prose (corrected 2026-09-07; commit `b4be65b`). A substitution anchored on a line break rather than a word boundary damaged words on four pages, including a heading; two pages also carried unexecuted replacement instructions containing both versions of the sentences. The prose was not compiled and no test read it, so every pipeline check was blind to the damage. Reading the affected pages against their intended wording exposed it.

The general check in all six faults was to compare a claim with evidence that did not inherit the assumption under test: independently interpreted states and epochs, published station offsets, a known-velocity orbit, the actual fit inputs, or the expected test inventory. The pipeline now applies that principle at each SpaceX fetch and covariance fit; the benchmark readers convert declared time systems and apply station eccentricities, the post-burn analysis calibrates the chord offset against clean reference arcs, regression tests pin those conventions, and CI compares completed tests with collected tests. These checks cannot expose errors shared by both sides, establish accuracy for catalogue objects without an independent reference, or recover tracking and fit provenance the source does not publish; prose still requires an independent reading ([frame check](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/ephemeris-frame.md), [fit guard](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/src/driftwatch/risk/covariance.py), [reference processing](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/src/driftwatch/storm/precise.py), [test-count guard](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/scripts/check_test_count.py)).

## 8. Limits

Everything above is indicative, not operational ([findings, closing statement](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/findings.md)). The covariances come from the consistency of public element sets, which bounds their accuracy in neither direction; the calibrations against reconstructed orbits cover fifteen spacecraft in five altitude bands and find that consistency under-covers the error in a storm, and at 1,338 km in the quiet week as well; the Kelvins reproduction validates the probability arithmetic on ESA's inputs and calibrates none of this; the probabilities are computed by the two-dimensional method, an underestimate for slow encounters. Absolute probabilities are indicative.

One week of sets per window and per mission, so a mission's horizon rests on a few dozen sets, and the exclusions of section 1 stand ([reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)). The storm term is tested against reconstructed orbits in four windows across the reference population. Skill on the live population is measured in May only, and skill against forecast ap has never been measured; calibration of the covariance around any event is also unmeasured ([storm-week test](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/storm-validation.md), [withdrawn headlines](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/findings.md#5-three-withdrawn-headlines) and [reference benchmark](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/reference-benchmark.md)). The comparisons with SpaceX's files are one lead bin of a six-bin table on nineteen satellites on one date, against the operator's published prediction rather than the realised orbit ([SpaceX comparison](https://github.com/nikhilb9244-cloud/driftwatch/blob/paper-2026-09-v1/docs/spacex-ephemerides.md)). The post-burn mechanism rests on twelve burns of six spacecraft in four windows. The radio lane ran on one observation in one period. No operator has used the tool, asked for it, or agreed to be named in connection with it; nobody has run the local path on files of their own; no conjunction data message and no operator ephemeris from outside the project has been through it.

## 9. What operational use would require

- A covariance scale per altitude band, fitted on the May 2024 window and validated on the held-out windows, for the storm under-coverage and the 1,338 km failure; recorded, not applied.
- A diagnosis of the storm term's quiet-condition baseline before any part of it is zeroed, and its skill measured on a second storm, on the live population and against reconstructed orbits.
- Calibration on the populations section 1 excludes.
- For the first set after a burn, a published manoeuvre record or the following set: the mark of section 5 is retrospective, and on a catalogue as it stands no following set exists yet.
- A conjunction data message and an operator ephemeris from outside the project through the matcher and the local path, which have run on constructed inputs only.
- An operator, and a validation of warning completeness against that operator's own record, before any probability on a page is read as more than indicative.

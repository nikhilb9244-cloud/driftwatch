# Public element sets against reconstructed orbits through the storms of 2024: what was measured, what was corrected, and what it does not show

_Draft for review, 8 September 2026. Every number below is taken from `docs/findings.md` as it stood on that date, with the item it derives from named beside it; nothing is measured here that is not measured there. Every correction carries the date on which it was made._

## 1. What was measured, and on what population

Every public element set issued inside a one-week window is one trial: it is propagated with SGP4 to leads from six hours to seven days and compared with the spacecraft's reconstructed orbit in the spacecraft's radial, in-track, cross-track frame, one residual per lead and never one per timestamp. The covariance carried beside each set and the ballistic coefficient the storm term uses are fitted from the 45 and 36 days of history before the window and from nothing inside it. Four windows: a control from 20 to 27 April 2024 with Kp at or under 4; the May 2024 storm, sets issued 6 to 13 May; the 10 to 11 October 2024 storm (Kp 9-), held out from every tuning; and the 12 August 2024 storm (Kp 8-), held out in the same way. No threshold was chosen by looking at October or August (items 6 and 8).

The population is fifteen spacecraft whose reconstructed orbit a server hands out without an account: Swarm A, B and C (ESA's SP3 product), GRACE-FO 1 and 2 (JPL Level-1B through GFZ), Sentinel-1A (Copernicus POEORB through ESA's STEP mirror), and the DORIS satellites CryoSat-2, SARAL, Sentinel-3A and 3B, SWOT, HY-2C and 2D, Jason-3 and Sentinel-6A (CNES POE through the IDS data centre), at 460 to 1,338 km, near-circular, free-flying between manoeuvres. Manoeuvre arcs are excluded from a published thruster record where one exists (Swarm, GRACE-FO) and from a step detector on the reconstructed orbit otherwise, with the 24 hours before each set's epoch counted on both paths. 1,201 element sets are scored. Laser-ranging normal points are a second reference for every mission with a retroreflector; the two references agree at the metre level, with median one-way range residuals of −0.1 to −0.6 m for every mission but SWOT, whose residual is a constant −2.0 m in every window (item 8). Nothing is measured for debris, eccentric orbits, station-kept constellations, objects the network tracks less often, or altitudes above 1,340 km (item 8).

The horizon is stated for one task: keeping the spacecraft inside the 25 km in-track half-width of the screening box at the 95th percentile of trials (item 8):

One correction to the table is dated 2026-09-07. The record path had from the first run dropped every set with a burn in the 24 hours before its epoch; the detection path had dropped only pairs whose propagation arc crossed a burn. The rule was extended to the detection path without change, after the held-out results had been seen. It moved the 750 to 850 km October horizon from 3 days to 5 and the 600 to 750 km August horizon from 6 days to 5; the 460 to 507 km row did not move; the usable sets went from 1,228 to 1,201. The held-out figures in the two bands carry that qualification, and both tables as they stood before the correction are kept on the pages (item 8).

## 2. Calibration: the storm under-coverage, and the separate failure at 1,338 km

The covariance is fitted from the consistency of an object's successive element sets and claims 95 per cent of residuals inside two sigma. On Swarm (item 6) it over-covers in the quiet week from one to five days, with 82 to 96 per cent of in-track residuals inside one sigma against the 68 claimed and 98 to 100 inside two, and under-covers inside twelve hours, with 37 to 49 inside one sigma and 60 to 75 inside two. In both storms it under-covers at every lead: two sigma contains 65 to 80 per cent of the May residuals and 62 to 75 per cent of the October ones, one sigma 33 to 76 and 31 to 63. On the fifteen spacecraft (item 8) the 460 to 507 km band repeats this, at 98 to 100 per cent inside two sigma in the quiet week from one to seven days, 65 to 84 per cent in the storms and 33 per cent at seven days in August; above 600 km the storms under-cover at a day, at 67 to 85 per cent; the six-hour under-coverage is general, at 17 to 57 per cent in every band.

The explanation recorded on 2026-09-05 (item 6) has two parts. A covariance fitted from the consistency of quiet fits cannot describe storm-time error, because nothing in how quiet fits disagree with one another says how far a storm will move the object; the benchmark's own fits are of that kind and under-cover in both storms. A second mechanism has a measured size: the fit excludes intervals that span a detected burn, the detector reads an unexplained change in semi-major axis as a burn, and storm drag is such a change. On the 3 September 2026 run, whose 45-day window held no three-hour interval at Kp 6 or above (its maximum was 5.7), none of the 17,033 excluded intervals coincided with one; in the May 2024 rows 155 of 4,617 excluded intervals coincided with Kp 6 or above, 145 of them on Starlinks, and of 17 free-flying objects whose history spans the 10 to 13 May storm, 2, both debris, had the storm interval excluded as a burn. Nothing in the detector was changed.

At 1,338 km the covariance fails in the quiet week as well, for a reason of its own (item 9), on Jason-3 and Sentinel-6A, in-track, inside two sigma:

| Window | Median in-track residual at a day | Fitted one-day sigma | Inside two sigma at a day | At six hours |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| quiet | 0.27 km | 0.26 km | 51 % | 32 % |
| May 2024 | 0.45 km | 0.11 km | 6 % | 15 % |
| October 2024 | 0.32 km | 0.43 km | 81 % | 53 % |

| 400 to 600 km, quiet, for comparison | 0.37 km | 0.98 km | 98 % | |

The sets agree with one another to less than their error. Drag is negligible at that altitude, so successive fits by the same network to the same tracking with the same force model repeat one another, and the error they share is invisible to any measure of their consistency. The fitted sigma states the network's repeatability, and it coincides with its accuracy only where a changing atmosphere makes successive fits disagree by about as much as they are wrong, which is what happens at 500 km in a quiet week and nowhere in a storm. At 1,338 km the quiet-scenario probability is therefore computed with an in-track sigma below the error in every window; the fix recorded, a covariance scale per altitude band validated on the held-out windows, is applied on no page (item 9). Two decisions were recorded on 2026-09-05 and not made: a quiet-condition baseline for the storm term, which wants a diagnosis before anything is zeroed, and a storm-conditional covariance scale, which must be fitted on May 2024 and validated on October 2024 or it is a tuning on the number it is meant to predict (item 6).

## 3. The storm term: skill by lead and by coefficient source, and the withdrawn headlines

The storm term is the in-track shift from the density excess over what a set's own drag term implies, driven by the observed ap and a ballistic coefficient. Its forecast test (item 4) propagates each object's last pre-storm set through the May 2024 storm and compares it with the sets issued during the storm, against a quiet control at the same leads, on the objects of the live catalogue; a comparison is validated only where both coefficients were measured from the objects' own decay, 101 objects and 498 comparisons on the redrawn sample (81 and 422 on the first). The term has skill only for that population and none where the coefficient is a B* inversion or a population stand-in: a B* coefficient regresses at slope −1.39, and the free-flying population as a whole is uncorrelated. The skill sits at three to four days of lead and is near zero inside two (recomputed 2026-09-05 and reproduced on a redrawn sample the same day): the observed sign agrees with the predicted one on 39 and 41 per cent of comparisons at one and two days on the first draw and 38 and 33 on the second, chance being 50, and on 91 and 96 per cent at three and four days (88 and 97 on the second); the robust slope is −0.15 and −0.04 inside two days against 0.63 and 0.71 beyond (−0.12 and −0.09 against 0.63 and 0.75). The quiet-time propagation error is 8 to 10 km at three days, and a predicted storm shift of 2 to 5 km at one or two days sits inside it. NRLMSIS 2.1 over-predicts the storm's three-day density enhancement by 22 to 23 per cent on the two draws over 450 to 2,000 km; recorded, not applied.

Against the reconstructed orbits (item 6, Swarm) the term with the observed ap reduces the May residual only from four days of lead, by 20, 42, 48 and 41 per cent on the median at four to seven days with 54 to 76 per cent of trials improved, and increases it from twelve hours to three days, by 64, 68, 287, 93 and 22 per cent; its magnitude is too large, a median predicted shift of 100 km at seven days against an actual 64. In the held-out October storm it helps from six hours to five days, by 5 to 43 per cent, and hurts at six and seven days, by 3 and 12. In the quiet control it makes the residual worse from one to six days, by 10 to 96 per cent, because the excess it integrates is not zero without a storm. On the fifteen spacecraft (item 8) the same holds at 460 to 507 km (−56 per cent at a day and −137 at three in the quiet week; May +14 and +35 per cent at three and seven days; October +41 at three; August +14 and +12); above 700 km its effect is within about 30 per cent either way with no consistent sign, and at 1,338 km it is nil at a day, because the coefficient it needs is fitted from decay that is not measurable there.

Corrected 2026-09-05 (item 4): the term must not be applied to operator-controlled objects, whose trajectory already carries the operator's drag model and planned burns. Before the correction every object with a coefficient was displaced, which put shifts of up to 31,000 km on Starlinks whose supplemental B* described a thrusting plan, 42 objects on the 1 September run and 36 on the 3 September one, explained at the time as a physical population in the densest shell. That explanation was wrong. Such objects now get no mean shift, and the 3 September run's forecast tally moved from 0 red, 16 yellow and 71 unscoreable to 1 red, 19 yellow and none.

Three headlines were withdrawn (item 5). "A storm lowers the probability on most events, because the two objects are displaced alike": on 2026-09-03 the diagnostic built to test the common-mode explanation found the relative displacement that reaches the miss to be a median 1.91 times the mean of the two objects' own displacements, out of a possible 2, because a conjunction is a crossing at a median 120 degrees between the two in-track directions; on 2026-09-05 the lowering itself, a median ratio of 0.16 to 0.40 on the validated events, was found to live in events with an operator-controlled side. On the 981 events of the 3 September run with both objects free-flying, the probability is lowered on 55 and raised on 43 under a G5 scenario, at a median ratio of 0.98, with a median relative displacement of 2 to 7 km against covariances of kilometres to tens of kilometres. "Screening on the operator's own published states gives the demo fleet its one red flag", corrected 2026-09-05: the flag, EOS SAT-1 against Starlink 61705 at 2.780 km and a fifteen-hour lead, at 1.076 × 10⁻⁴ against 6.19 × 10⁻⁶ on the catalogue's fit, is in the dilution region at low confidence, its maximum probability over covariance scale factors sitting at 0.85 times the covariance in hand; it is not a warning to act on. "The storm term is predictive at r = 0.88 where the coefficient is measured", withdrawn 2026-09-05: a redrawn sample gave 0.64; the four objects the two draws shared agree to the third decimal, so the code did not move; the correlation is carried by the two per cent of predictions with the most weight, and dropping them takes 0.88 to 0.68 and 0.64 to 0.55. What reproduced between the draws is the robust slope (0.65, then 0.68), the sign agreement at three and four days, the absence of skill inside two days and the density over-prediction (22 per cent, then 23). No correlation is quoted.

## 4. The dSGP4 held-out result

ESA's dsgp4 (Acciarini, Baydin and Izzo 2025; version 1.3.0) was run on the 1,201 usable trial sets (item 10). With WGS72 constants it reproduces the sgp4 library's in-track residual to the metre at every lead in every window; with its default WGS-84 constants it differs by up to five per cent of the median. Its ML-dSGP4 hybrid wraps the propagator in two networks that perturb the mean elements before the propagation and the state after it, trained against a higher-precision reference by the mean squared error of the normalised state; the published training used an operator's predictions in a quiet week, and the storm result had not been published. Two hybrids were trained on the quiet and May windows only, with the corrections starting at zero so that the untrained model is SGP4 and the epoch of lowest training loss kept: one on Swarm (111 sets, 18,648 hourly truth states to seven days) and one on every mission with a reconstructed orbit (625 sets, 105,000 states). A sweep of the learning rate over 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵ on the Swarm set gave losses of 6.66, 6.73 and 6.77 × 10⁻⁶ against 6.80 at the zero start: the training lowers the published objective and raises the residual. On the training windows the Swarm hybrid's median in-track residual is 7 to 46 km against SGP4's 0.4 to 7 km, the all-mission hybrid's 1.5 to 7 km; on the held-out storms neither improves one of the ten leads, at a day in October 15 and 5 km against 0.64, in August 14 and 7 km against 0.45, at seven days in October 92 and 16 km against 11. The rule fixed before the numbers were seen, adopt only if the held-out storms improve, says do not adopt. The objective is a mean of squared errors over every hour to seven days, dominated by the seven-day tail of tens to hundreds of kilometres, so its optimum trades the short leads for a change at the tail: a loss that fell by two per cent moved the day-one residual by an order of magnitude. This does not say the hybrid cannot be made to help; it says the published recipe, on public element sets against reconstructed orbits, did not.

## 5. The post-burn mechanism

In the first run of the expansion Sentinel-3B's October horizon was 3 days against Sentinel-3A's 7 in the same orbit. The cause was the exclusion rule of section 1, corrected 2026-09-07: every short 3B window was carried by the one set issued a few hours after that window's burn (10 October 10:20 UTC, an hour after a burn ending 09:25, 33 km at four days; 25 April 14:38, 25 km; 13 August 22:11, 30 km), and with 7 to 10 usable sets per window one such set is the 95th percentile. Why 3B's first post-burn sets were wrong by tens of kilometres and 3A's, after burns of the same kind, were not was recorded as open (item 8).

The property the two differ in, the cadence of their sets (one every 6.7 hours for 3A against one every 10 to 13 hours for 3B), was tested on 2026-09-08 on every mission with a burn inside a window's set span and a set issued after it: twelve burns on six spacecraft at 700 to 950 km, Sentinel-1A, CryoSat-2, Sentinel-3A, Sentinel-3B, SWOT and HY-2D, in all four windows, each burn placed by the orbit-step detector to about an orbit either side; the recorded burns of Swarm and GRACE-FO fell after their windows' sets. The rank correlation of the first post-burn set's in-track residual at four days with cadence is +0.78 per burn (n = 12, p = 0.003), and it is Sentinel-3B, whose three burns are the three sparsest cadences and the three largest residuals (25, 30 and 33 km); without them it is +0.48 on nine burns (p = 0.19) and with the spacecraft as the unit +0.66 on six (p = 0.16). Error does not scale with cadence on this population. The delay from the burn to the first set's epoch holds at every level (−0.71 per burn, −0.62 without 3B, −0.89 per spacecraft, p = 0.019): the seven first sets issued within ten hours of the burn are wrong at four days by 2.3 to 33 km and the five issued twelve hours or later by 0.1 to 2.3 km, on twelve burns of six spacecraft in four windows, a split re-measured whenever a window is added; the second set after every burn is within 5.3 km. Burn size, 9 to 95 m of semi-major axis, orders nothing.

The mechanism was found the same day by asking what fit each set carries (item 8). Each set's semi-major axis was put in the reconstructed orbit's convention, SGP4 over one revolution centred on its epoch with the osculating value averaged as the detector averages the orbit's; the constant between the two conventions, 70 to 74 m for every mission and most of it the ten-second finite-difference velocity the orbit reader carries, was calibrated on each window's sets with no burn in their arc, whose scatter is 2 to 6 m; and the set's error was measured against the orbit at its own epoch, because CryoSat-2 at 717 km decays about 10 m a day and had moved 25 m below the post-burn plateau by the time its sets were issued. The fraction of the burn a set contains is one plus its error over the burn. The thresholds, a quarter and three quarters, and the rule that a burn under three scatters is unresolved, were fixed before any set was classified.

| Spacecraft | Burns | Fraction of the burn in the first set after it | Class |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Sentinel-3B | 3 | 0.06, 0.04, 0.12 | pre-burn fit, epoch advanced past the burn |
| SWOT | 1 | −0.07 | pre-burn fit, epoch advanced past the burn |
| CryoSat-2 | 3 | 0.92, 1.06, 0.85 | contains the burn |
| Sentinel-3A | 2 | 1.02, 0.98 | contains the burn |
| HY-2D | 2 | 0.86, 1.00 | contains the burn |
| Sentinel-1A | 1 | −0.22 | unresolved: a 9 m burn against a 15 m threshold |

No set lies between the two classes. For the four re-epoched sets the drift their error predicts, three halves of the mean motion times the error times the lead, reproduces the observed residual at four days to within 20 per cent (−21, −34, −25 and −17 km predicted against −25, −33, −30 and −19 observed) and its growth with lead; the 0.5 to 3 km it falls short by is present at a day, the offset the set carries at its epoch. The seven sets that contain the burn have errors of −7 to +5 m against the orbit at their epoch; Sentinel-1A's −11 m predicts its residual (−1.5 km at a day and −6 at four against −1.5 and −4.2 observed), as a pre-burn fit of a 9 m burn would. So, on twelve burns of six spacecraft in four windows: the first set after a burn is wrong by the part of the burn it does not contain, and whether it contains the burn or is a pre-burn fit re-epoched past it differs by object, not by delay. CryoSat-2's set issued 5.4 hours after its burn contained it; Sentinel-3B's issued 3 to 8 hours after did not. The ten-hour split describes this sample, in which the re-epoched sets were the ones issued within ten hours. What stays open is why the network re-epochs a pre-burn fit for some objects and refits after the burn for others; the sets do not say. Nothing in a re-epoched set reveals what it is; the jump appears only when a later set contains the burn, so a per-object mark of the condition is retrospective (`docs/radio-lane.md`).

## 6. The radio horizons: crossing and position

The radio lane (item 7) converts the benchmark's residuals into angles at the MeerKAT array centre and tests them against a third of the primary beam's half-power width, per altitude band of section 1, each object scored against its own band's trials. The crossing horizon, governed by the cross-track error, answers whether an object crossed the beam during an observation: the cross-track residual stays under 3.4 arcmin at the 95th percentile at every lead in every window in every band, so the crossing horizon is the benchmark's seven days in every band and window at every receiver, with one exception, 5 days at 600 to 750 km in August 2024; the crossing's time is known to the along-track shift, 0.2 s at the 95th percentile at six hours at 400 to 600 km and 8, 25, 72 and 29 s at seven days in the quiet, May, October and August windows. The position horizon, governed by the along-track error, answers where an object is at an instant to within a third of the beam, and it is the one a storm moves and altitude lengthens. At the L-band centre:

| Band | quiet | May 2024 | October 2024 | August 2024 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 400 to 600 km | 24 h | 12 h | 6 h | 12 h |
| 600 to 750 km | 24 h | 24 h | 12 h | 36 h |
| 750 to 850 km | 6 d | 2 d | 6 h | 3 d |
| 850 to 1,000 km | 7 d | 7 d | 3 d | 7 d |
| 1,338 km | 7 d | 3 d | 7 d | 7 d |

S-band position prediction from public element sets is not possible at any element-set age at 400 to 600 km: at the top of the band the position horizon is under six hours, the benchmark's shortest lead, in every window. Higher it is possible: at the top of S band, 12 hours in the quiet and May windows at 600 to 750 km, 3 days in the quiet week at 750 to 850 km, seven days in the quiet week at 850 to 1,000 km with 12 hours to 4 days in the storms, and seven days in every window but May (36 hours) at 1,338 km. Both horizons rest on the population of section 1; every object outside it carries no measured horizon for either.

## 7. The format and provenance faults, as a class

Six faults of one class were found: in the naming of a frame, of a time system, of a fit's inputs, in the realisation of a reference frame, in a file's construction, and in a trajectory's origin. Each was invisible to every check that compared a source with itself, and each was exposed by a comparison with something the source had not produced.

## 8. Limits

Everything above is indicative, not operational (findings, closing statement). The covariances come from the consistency of public element sets, which measures how much successive fits by one network disagree and bounds their accuracy in neither direction; the calibrations against reconstructed orbits cover fifteen spacecraft in five altitude bands and find that consistency under-covers the error in a storm, and at 1,338 km in the quiet week as well; the Kelvins reproduction validates the probability arithmetic on ESA's inputs and calibrates none of this; and the probabilities are computed by the two-dimensional method, an underestimate for slow encounters. Absolute probabilities are indicative.

One week of sets per window and per mission, so a mission's horizon rests on a few dozen sets. Nothing is measured for debris, eccentric orbits, station-kept constellations, objects the network tracks less often, or above 1,340 km, and a detection that reads storm drag as a burn removes trials from the intervals a storm benchmark needs, a failure mode counted for Swarm and not measured for the detection-only missions (item 8). The storm term is measured on one storm's forecast test and against the reconstructed orbits of one orbit class in two storms; its skill on a second storm, its calibration on the live population, the direction in which a storm moves a free-flying event's probability, and the covariance around any event are not measured (item 5). The comparisons with SpaceX's files are one lead bin of a six-bin table on nineteen satellites on one date, against the operator's published prediction rather than the realised orbit (item 1). The post-burn mechanism rests on twelve burns of six spacecraft in four windows, and the ten-hour split describes that sample. The radio lane ran on one observation in one period; the other period had no public observation record to run on. No operator has used the tool, asked for it, or agreed to be named in connection with it; nobody has run the local path on files of their own; no conjunction data message and no operator ephemeris from outside the project has been through it. The two decisions of section 2 stand open.

## 9. What operational use would require

- A covariance scale per altitude band, fitted on the May 2024 window and validated on the held-out windows, for the storm under-coverage and the 1,338 km failure of section 2; recorded, not applied.
- A diagnosis of the storm term's quiet-condition baseline before any part of it is zeroed, and its skill measured on a second storm, on the live population and against reconstructed orbits.
- Calibration on the populations section 1 excludes: debris, eccentric orbits, station-kept objects, objects the network tracks less often, altitudes above 1,340 km.
- For the first set after a burn, a published manoeuvre record or the following set: the mark of section 5 is retrospective, and on a catalogue as it stands no following set exists yet.
- A conjunction data message and an operator ephemeris from outside the project through the matcher and the local path, which have run on constructed inputs only.
- An operator, and a validation of warning completeness against that operator's own record, before any probability on a page is read as more than indicative.

## Abstract

Public element-set errors vary sharply with altitude, disturbance and mission. For spacecraft at {{publication/low_altitude_min_km|.0f}}–{{publication/low_altitude_max_km|.0f}} km, last-pass/first-fail brackets are {{publication/low_horizons/quiet/last_lead_h_within|g}}/{{publication/low_horizons/quiet/first_lead_h_beyond|g}} hours in {{publication/windows/quiet}}, {{publication/low_horizons/storm/last_lead_h_within|g}}/{{publication/low_horizons/storm/first_lead_h_beyond|g}} in {{publication/windows/storm}}, {{publication/low_horizons/august/last_lead_h_within|g}}/{{publication/low_horizons/august/first_lead_h_beyond|g}} in {{publication/windows/august}} and {{publication/low_horizons/held-out/last_lead_h_within|g}}/{{publication/low_horizons/held-out/first_lead_h_beyond|g}} in {{publication/windows/held-out}}. Whole-set deletion leaves these brackets unchanged; spacecraft deletion preserves all except the {{publication/windows/august}} bracket. Bands above {{publication/high_band_boundary_km|g}} km pass through the longest tested lead, with mission-dependent fragility. Consistency covariance fails to cover storm residuals and {{missions/sentinel-6a/name}} even in the control. The September replay gives mean absolute prediction error {{extras/locked/result/primary/all_recorded_burns/mean_absolute_error_prediction_km|.3f}} km against {{extras/locked/result/primary/all_recorded_burns/mean_absolute_error_zero_km|.3f}} km for zero prediction; {{publication/n_beats_zero}} of {{extras/locked/result/primary/all_recorded_burns/n_complete}} events improve, with {{missions/swot/name}} and one {{missions/sentinel-3a/name}} event as the exceptions. Constructed topocentric crossings agree in {{publication/radio_strata/Other declared offsets/agreement_fraction|.1%}} of cases away from the deliberate boundary offsets and {{publication/radio_strata/Exact boundary offsets/agreement_fraction|.1%}} at them. Both learned-propagator checkpoints are rejected. These results describe inspected populations, not transferable operating guarantees.

## Population and comparison method

Building on [Flohrer, Krag and Klinkrad ({{publication/reference_years/flohrer}})](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2008/Orbital_Debris/Flohrer.pdf), who estimated covariance from element-set consistency, this work measures component coverage against reconstructed orbits with covariance and residuals expressed in the same basis. Extending [Mason's ({{publication/reference_years/mason}})](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0842) NASA Ames comparison of element sets with reference ephemerides, it reports mission-specific storm and control comparisons, recorded-manoeuvre exclusions and deletion-sensitive horizon brackets. Alongside [Parker and Linares ({{publication/reference_years/parker}})](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.A36164), who examined catalogue decay and geomagnetic forecast performance during the Gannon storm, it measures propagation residuals against reconstructed orbits without interpreting observed storm conditions as issued forecasts. Following [Acciarini, Baydin and Izzo ({{publication/reference_years/acciarini}})](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2024.10.063), who introduced differentiable and learned SGP4 propagation, it records paired negative findings for the local checkpoints and public-data recipe rather than claiming to reproduce their experiment.

We compare public SGP4 element sets with reconstructed reference orbits for {{population/n_missions}} spacecraft in {{population/n_bands}} altitude bands. The sample spans {{population/minimum_altitude_km|,.0f}}–{{population/maximum_altitude_km|,.0f}} km and contains {{population/n_raw_sets|,}} raw element sets. Of these, {{population/n_usable_sets_any_lead|,}} contribute a usable comparison at some lead, yielding {{population/n_usable_pairs|,}} set/lead pairs. Each pair compares propagation from an element-set epoch with the reference state at the same time. These are epoch-based reconstructions; state epochs do not establish historical publication or availability.

The windows are {{publication/windows/quiet}}, {{publication/windows/storm}}, {{publication/windows/august}} and {{publication/windows/held-out}}. All have been inspected. The retained “held out” names describe their historical roles; the corrected comparisons are descriptive, and none of these windows remains an untouched evaluation population. The supplement records the exact epoch selections and mission composition at each lead.

Published manoeuvre records govern exclusions where integrated. A recorded burn from {{definition/pre_epoch_manoeuvre_arc_h|g}} hours before the element-set epoch through the comparison time excludes that pair. This span is an analysis rule, not a measured catalogue fitting arc. Missing record coverage is not evidence of no burn. Orbit and element-set detectors provide cross-checks on recorded missions and explicitly identified exclusions elsewhere. The IDS registry does not independently establish exhaustive reporting of every firing.

The reference products come from the ESA Swarm, JPL/GFZ GRACE-FO, CNES/SSALTO and Sentinel precise-orbit archives cited in the supplement. GRACE-FO adds another mission, producer and thruster record, although these and Swarm remain related GNSS-derived references. Laser ranging supplies a different measurement on sampled passes. Agreement in range helps check conventions and reference points; it does not measure the complete spatial tail distribution. Debris, eccentric orbits and unmeasured catalogue objects are outside this population.

## Residual horizons

A lead passes when at least {{definition/quantile|.0%}} of finite, usable absolute in-track residuals lie within {{definition/tolerance_km|g}} km. The bracket joins the last passing bin to the first failing bin; it does not locate a continuous transition between them. Passing through the longest tested lead, {{publication/longest_lead_h|g}} hours, is right censoring. Missing observations end the supported interval separately from a measured threshold failure. Empirical counts determine the primary result; linear and inverted-CDF quantiles remain alongside them in the supplement.

{{table:horizons}}

The low-altitude brackets survive deletion of any whole element set. Removing a spacecraft preserves the {{publication/windows/quiet}}, {{publication/windows/storm}} and {{publication/windows/held-out}} brackets; in {{publication/windows/august}} it can move the bracket earlier. The intermediate bands are more sensitive to who remains in the sample. In {{publication/windows/storm}}, the {{missions/cryosat-2/altitude_band}} band passes through the longest tested lead with only {{results/by_band/600-750 km/storm/by_lead_h/168/n}} {{missions/cryosat-2/name}} sets remaining. In {{publication/windows/august}}, its next lead has no usable observations. Above {{publication/high_band_boundary_km|g}} km, the pooled bands pass through the longest tested lead, but the highest band in {{publication/windows/storm}} can fail there after a spacecraft deletion.

Successive fits share tracking data; leads reuse trajectories; missions share orbit producers and storm conditions. The deletion checks describe fragility under those dependencies. They are not confidence intervals, and the selected disturbed windows cannot supply a sampling distribution for future storms.

## September calendar replay

The pre-registered endpoint was signed in-track error {{publication/gracefo_d/propagation_age_hours|g}} hours after the first post-manoeuvre element-set epoch. The locally frozen protocol yielded {{extras/locked/result/primary/all_recorded_burns/n_complete}} complete recorded-burn predictor/endpoints, with mean absolute prediction error {{extras/locked/result/primary/all_recorded_burns/mean_absolute_error_prediction_km|.3f}} km against {{extras/locked/result/primary/all_recorded_burns/mean_absolute_error_zero_km|.3f}} km for the zero-prediction baseline. The energy predictor beat that baseline in {{publication/n_beats_zero}} of {{extras/locked/result/primary/all_recorded_burns/n_complete}} events; SWOT and one {{missions/sentinel-3a/name}} event did not.

The failures matter. GRACE-FO D has a calibrated burn fraction of {{publication/gracefo_d/fraction|.3f}} and a {{publication/gracefo_d/absolute_prediction_error_km|.2f}} km prediction error. It still improves on its {{publication/gracefo_d/absolute_zero_error_km|.2f}} km zero-baseline error, but that large residual limits the diagnostic. SWOT's event on {{publication/swot/event_date}} has the wrong sign: the prediction is {{publication/swot/predicted_in_track_km|+.3f}} km and the observed residual is {{publication/swot/signed_in_track_km|+.3f}} km. Its {{publication/swot/absolute_prediction_error_km|.3f}} km prediction error exceeds the {{publication/swot/absolute_zero_error_km|.3f}} km baseline. The other baseline failure is {{missions/sentinel-3a/name}} on {{publication/sentinel3a_failure/event_date}}, with a {{publication/sentinel3a_failure/absolute_prediction_error_km|.3f}} km prediction error against {{publication/sentinel3a_failure/absolute_zero_error_km|.3f}} km for the zero baseline.

Calibration uses only earlier clean sets. Every published in-month burn remains in the inventory, including events with an intervening burn. The predeclared subset without another recorded burn has {{extras/locked/result/primary/without_intervening_recorded_burn/n_complete}} complete endpoints. The mean prediction error stays below the zero baseline in all {{extras/locked/publication_diagnostics/n_spacecraft_deletions}} spacecraft deletions.

The event inventory is not an independent sample. Its {{extras/locked/result/primary/all_recorded_burns/n_events}} rows share {{extras/locked/publication_diagnostics/n_unique_post_event_endpoints}} distinct endpoints. Closely spaced {{missions/sentinel-3b/name}} burns overlap the fixed energy plateaus; the no-later-burn subset does not isolate the earlier plateau. An intermediate calibrated fraction therefore does not establish mixed catalogue fitting for an isolated manoeuvre.

The protocol and separate author attestation preceded experiment access locally; this is not externally timestamped preregistration. Earlier public-element and weather history and overlapping orbit products already existed. This replay is a retrospective physical diagnostic, not a prediction shown to have been available at element-set publication. September will not be reused as a hold-out for any new recipe. The [status page](protocols/september-2024-execution-status.md) and [complete event tables](locked-benchmark-v2-tables.md) retain chronology, exclusions, unresolved classifications and source-retention limits.

## Component covariance and reference checks

Consistency covariance measures disagreement between catalogue solutions. It can miss shared error or include independent fit noise, so it bounds absolute accuracy in neither direction. We transport the predicted-state RIC covariance into each residual's truth RIC basis before extracting component sigmas. Component intervals are distinct from joint spatial ellipsoid coverage; this paper reports the former.

{{table:components}}

The table illustrates why coverage must be stated by component and mission. In-track intervals fail during storms, while even the control can fail in another component. At the same nominal high altitude, {{missions/jason-3/name}} passes the {{publication/sigma_multiple}}σ in-track check for {{results/by_mission/jason-3/quiet/by_lead_h/24/components/in_track/coverage/inside_2_sigma_count}}/{{results/by_mission/jason-3/quiet/by_lead_h/24/components/in_track/coverage/n}} sets while {{missions/sentinel-6a/name}} fails completely, {{results/by_mission/sentinel-6a/quiet/by_lead_h/24/components/in_track/coverage/inside_2_sigma_count}}/{{results/by_mission/sentinel-6a/quiet/by_lead_h/24/components/in_track/coverage/n}}; the latter's fitted median sigma is {{results/by_mission/sentinel-6a/quiet/by_lead_h/24/components/in_track/coverage/median_sigma_km|.3f}} km, about a tenth of its {{results/by_mission/sentinel-6a/quiet/by_lead_h/24/components/in_track/median_abs_km|.3f}} km median residual, with no cause established by this comparison.

The storm term is outside this paper's scope; its coefficient support, residuals and shift diagnostics remain in [the benchmark tables](benchmark-v2-tables.md) and [storm-term analysis](storm-validation.md).

## Topocentric crossing agreement

An orbital-component angle is not a sky-track displacement, and dividing in-track error by orbital speed is not a beam-crossing time error. We instead compare complete topocentric trajectories with a rotating observer and fixed celestial pointings against the measured MeerKAT beams. The cases are constructed pointings on previously inspected windows. They do not represent an observing schedule or detected interference.

Of {{extras/radio_tracks/result/n_input_trials|,}} source-filtered rows, {{extras/radio_tracks/result/n_eligible_trials}} have qualifying nominal visibility and complete curves. These yield {{extras/radio_tracks/result/n_cases|,}} beam/pointing/observation cases from {{extras/radio_tracks_diagnostics/result/n_sets}} element sets on {{extras/radio_tracks_diagnostics/result/n_missions}} spacecraft. Nominal eligibility excludes reference-visible events whose predictions fail the gate. Repeated beams, offsets and intervals do not create independent trajectories.

For the full observation interval, the other declared offsets agree in {{publication/radio_strata/Other declared offsets/agreement_count|,}}/{{publication/radio_strata/Other declared offsets/n_cases|,}} cases, with {{publication/radio_strata/Other declared offsets/false}}/{{publication/radio_strata/Other declared offsets/predicted|,}} false predicted crossings and {{publication/radio_strata/Other declared offsets/missed}}/{{publication/radio_strata/Other declared offsets/reference|,}} missed reference crossings. At the deliberately exact half-power boundary, agreement falls to {{publication/radio_strata/Exact boundary offsets/agreement_count}}/{{publication/radio_strata/Exact boundary offsets/n_cases|,}}. This construction-based split is a sensitivity check, separate from numerical near-boundary diagnostics.

{{table:radio_age}}

Each populated age bin has a different spacecraft composition, so these agreements do not establish an increase in agreement with element-set age.

Age bins are measured from element-set epoch, not publication. Agreement includes both crossing and non-crossing cases. Closest-time errors use all finite uncensored paired closest times, including false and missed crossings; entry and exit errors in the supplement require suitable crossings on both tracks. The table therefore keeps classification denominators beside timing errors. Receiver response, received power, coherence and real monitoring outcomes remain unmeasured. The measured beam data are {{publication/beam_licence}} and are used for research only.

## Rejected learned propagators

The {{publication/learned/n_models}} local learned-propagator checkpoints lower the pooled paired median in {{publication/learned/n_lower_median}}/{{publication/learned/n_cells}} model/window/lead cells and lower the interpolated tail in {{publication/learned/n_lower_tail}}/{{publication/learned/n_cells}}. Both are rejected by the stored adoption rule. Some individual spacecraft improve while tails worsen. These are negative findings for the stated checkpoints, population and recipe, not a rejection of learned propagation as a field.

This is a local adaptation of the published dSGP4 method. Checkpoints were selected by the fixed training objective and checked again after reload. The evaluation windows were already inspected before correction; their historical names do not make this rerun confirmatory. Training traces, paired denominators, recipes and checkpoint identities remain in [the supplement](benchmark-v2-tables.md#revised-learned-propagator-evaluation).

## Corrections and reproducibility

The covariance-basis correction dated {{publication/correction_date}} changes coverage counts in {{covariance_basis_correction/reference/n_coverage_cells_changed}}/{{covariance_basis_correction/reference/n_cells}} original benchmark cells and {{covariance_basis_correction/september/n_coverage_cells_changed}}/{{covariance_basis_correction/september/n_cells}} September secondary cells. All affected coverage counts are radial. Residuals, exclusions, in-track coverage counts and horizons are unchanged. [Every cell's change](covariance-basis-correction.md) is retained alongside the earlier manoeuvre-record and statistical corrections. The frozen September outputs remain intact; the supplement identifies the later covariance amendment.

The earlier post-manoeuvre classifications describe calibrated energy compatibility, not the catalogue's unobserved fitting procedure. Their thresholds were author-reported as chosen before execution, but the first commit containing them also contained results. The later dated classifier freeze cannot establish earlier preregistration. State epoch spacing does not establish publication cadence.

The evidence object binds input files, method definitions, source hashes and completed-result metadata. Prose is authored in a Markdown template; only figures and tables are substituted from evidence. Tests reject unbound figures or stale rendered text. [Rebuild instructions](publication-rebuild.md) specify the retained inputs and checks. The [complete tables](benchmark-v2-tables.md) hold hashes, checkpoints, timestamps and detailed references; the [archived earlier paper](archive/paper-2026-09-v1.md) preserves the superseded record.

Independent checks exposed clock, frame, fitting-window and station-reference-point errors that internally consistent calculations had missed. Those checks support the stated comparisons. They do not establish independent orbit determination, calibrated operational collision probabilities, warning completeness or a general radio operating horizon. Short-lead timing applications also require the open Cartesian dynamical validation recorded in the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md).
